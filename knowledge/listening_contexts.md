# Listening Contexts

How common listening situations tend to map to audio features. These are general
tendencies to guide retrieval and explanation, not prescriptions. People enjoy many
kinds of music in every context, and none of these are health or wellness claims.

## studying
Studying and focus usually pair well with low energy, slower tempo, higher
acousticness, and instrumental (no-lyric) tracks so words do not compete with
reading or writing. Lofi, ambient, and classical are common choices. This is about
reducing distraction, not a claim about improving grades or cognition.

## working out
Workouts usually pair well with high energy, fast tempo, and high danceability to
help maintain momentum. Pop, EDM, rock, and metal are common choices. Preference for
intensity varies widely between people and activities.

## relaxing
Relaxing usually pairs well with low energy, gentle tempo, warm acousticness, and
soothing moods. Ambient, jazz, folk, and soul are common choices. "Relaxing" here
means subjectively calming, not a treatment for stress or any medical condition.

## commuting
Commuting is flexible: some people want upbeat energy to wake up, others want calm
music to decompress. Moderate energy and familiar, steady tempos are common. Context
(driving, transit, walking) changes what feels appropriate.

## reflective listening
Reflective or late-night listening often pairs with lower energy, lower valence, and
moody or melancholic tones. Synthwave, ambient, jazz, and slow acoustic tracks are
common choices. This describes an aesthetic mood, not an emotional diagnosis.

## social settings
Social settings and parties usually pair with higher energy, higher valence, and high
danceability to keep a lively atmosphere. Pop, EDM, reggae, and indie pop are common
choices. The right feel depends heavily on the group and occasion.
